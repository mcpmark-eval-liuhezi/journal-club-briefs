# Surgery Journal Club — Evidence Brief

## Pilonidal Sinus Surgery: What Does the Evidence Say?

### Citation

Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020). Tokocin, Yigitbas, Yavuz, Gulcicek. *International Wound Journal*. 2026. **PMID: 42633928**.

*(Citation verified directly against the PubMed record for PMID 42633928.)*

### Plain-language summary

This is a large single-centre study from a Turkish tertiary referral centre that looked back at 2,222 patients who had surgery for a first-time (primary) pilonidal sinus between 2006 and 2020, with everyone followed for at least 5 years. The authors compared nine different surgical techniques to see which ones were best at preventing the sinus coming back (recurrence) and at avoiding postoperative infection. Overall, about 1 in 10 patients had a recurrence over the whole follow-up period (10.2%), with the 5-year recurrence rate at 7.9% and postoperative infection at 6.9%, and outcomes clearly differed between techniques. The lowest recurrence rates were seen with flap-based procedures — the Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%) and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%) — and after adjusting for patient factors these flaps were all independently associated with a substantially lower recurrence risk than simply laying the wound open (hazard ratios roughly 0.19–0.32), whereas a higher body mass index increased the risk. Most recurrences happened within the first two years, with a typical time to recurrence of about 20 months, which is useful for counselling patients about follow-up.

### Key numbers at a glance

| Outcome | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Patients (primary PSD, single centre, 2006–2020, ≥5-year follow-up) | 2,222 |
| 5-year cumulative recurrence | 7.9% (175/2222) |
| Overall recurrence | 10.2% (226/2222) |
| Postoperative infection | 6.9% (154/2222) |
| Lowest recurrence techniques | Karydakis flap 1.5%; Bascom cleft lift 2.3%; Dufourmentel flap 2.4% |
| Median time to recurrence | 20 months (IQR 12–36), most within first 2 years |
| Difference between techniques | Statistically significant for both recurrence and infection (both p < 0.001) |
